TL;DR

ARCTOS is a vision-guided sorting cell. An overhead camera identifies objects in a pick-up area, and a 6-DOF arm on a linear rail motion-plans to sort them into bins.

I lead the team. I planned the control architecture, I'm designing the linear rail in Onshape, and I work on low-level arm control in C++ over CAN bus.

The project is in progress. This page will add results as the cell starts sorting.

01

Problem and constraints

The cell has to find objects in a pick-up area and sort each one into the right bin, with no one guiding the arm.

  • The arm. It's based on the open-source Arctos design: 3D-printed, with cycloidal and planetary gearboxes, a 600 mm reach, and a 1 kg payload.
  • The team. Members come from mechanical, electrical, software, and biomedical engineering. Each software system needed a clear owner.
  • The rail. The linear rail extends the arm's workspace, so its tolerances and fits matter.

Credit: the arm design comes from the open-source Arctos project at arctosrobotics.com. The rail, control architecture, software, and sorting cell are the team's work.

What I built

Mechanical

  • Linear rail. I'm designing the linear rail in Onshape, including its tolerances and fits.
  • CAD teaching. I teach CAD to underclassmen on the team.

Electronics & Embedded

  • Low-level arm control. I work on arm control in C++ on Arduino, talking to the arm over CAN bus.

Controls & Software

  • Control architecture. I planned the architecture: two compute boards, a ThinkPad and a control board, plus a Raspberry Pi for vision. I assigned an owner to each software system.
  • Integration. The systems are integrated through ROS 2 (Lyrical).
  • Higher-level scripts. Higher-level behavior is written in MATLAB and Python.
  • Digital twin. We have a MuJoCo digital twin of the arm simulated in NVIDIA Isaac Sim.

Leadership

I lead a multidisciplinary team of mechanical, electrical, software, and biomedical engineering students.
