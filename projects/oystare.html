TL;DR

Oystare is a portable autonomous twin-hull rover that maps oyster reefs in shallow Massachusetts coastal water, built for a shellfish habitat restoration specialist.

I own propulsion and the camera probe. I ran the stakeholder interviews that refocused the project from water quality to reef mapping, and I wrote mechanical requirements traced to acceptance tests.

Water testing hasn't happened yet, so results below are placeholders.

01

Problem and constraints

Our client restores shellfish habitat and needs maps of oyster reefs in shallow coastal water. The project started as a water quality rover. I ran three stakeholder interviews, and what we learned refocused it on reef mapping.

I wrote the mechanical constraints and traced each one to an acceptance test.

What I built

Mechanical

Camera probe deployment

I ran a trade study of four ways to deploy the camera:

  • Fixed hull mount
  • Tethered winch
  • Telescoping pole
  • Towed sled

We visited a marina site in Wellfleet and ran drag and camera-depth tests. Based on those tests, the team chose a rigid mast over a lead-screw design.

Electronics & Embedded

  • Propulsion. Two Blue Robotics T100 thrusters, one per hull.
  • Probe power. I moved the camera probe to a 12 V input.

Controls & Software

The team's navigation stack uses GPS waypoint navigation on a Pixhawk flight controller running ArduPilot Rover. A Raspberry Pi companion computer runs ROS 2, and a Kalman filter handles state estimation.
